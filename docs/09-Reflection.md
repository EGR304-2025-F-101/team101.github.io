# Reflection

## Lessons Learned  

1. **Lesson 1:** We learned that figuring out our user needs early and backing them up with simple research such as reading Amazon reviews and comparing other products has helped us stay focused on real problems such as water waste, easy setup, and making the system reliable.
2. **Lesson 2:** We found out that keeping our design modular was a huge help. Splitting the project into four subsystems made it easier to track down issues because we could test each board on its own instead of guessing where a problem came from.
3. **Lesson 3:** Our design review taught us that simple straightforward documentation matters just as much as the actual hardware. If things weren’t explained well it can make even good decisions hard for others to understand.
4. **Lesson 4:** We learned how important regular team communication is because each person was responsible for a different subsystem and we all had to make sure our boards, pins, and timing lined up correctly.
5. **Lesson 5:** Testing the sensors early showed us that raw sensor readings don’t always behave the way you expect. We had to smooth, calibrate, and adjust values to get reliable soil moisture and light readings.
6. **Lesson 6:** We realized that power and grounding between different boards takes more planning than we thought. Each subsystem could only work reliably when everything shared a stable common ground.
7. **Lesson 7:** We learned the value of fixing things step-by-step. Every small improvement—whether it was adjusting the motor circuit, cleaning up the speaker signals, or reorganizing our block diagram—made the whole system run better.
8. **Lesson 8:** Working with real hardware helped us with fixing things like noise, loose connections, and how outdoor conditions affect the design a lot more than the simple ideas we started with on paper.
9. **Lesson 9:** We saw how important organized documentation and version control are mainly because when everyone is working on separate code files and hardware setups that all have to connect in the end.
10. **Lesson 10:** Finally, we learned what it’s like to build a full embedded system from start to finish—brainstorming, planning, building, testing, and bringing everything together, which helped us grow our electronic building skills and teamwork during our time in the class.

## Recommendations for Future Students  

1. **Recommendation 1:** Learn how to break a project into modular subsystems early because having clearly defined roles and clear electrical interfaces between boards will save a huge amount of time during debugging and integration.
2. **Recommendation 2:** Get comfortable with basic microcontroller skills—ADC reading, digital I/O, PWM, interrupts, and simple communication—because nearly every part of this class depends on applying those fundamentals quickly.
3. **Recommendation 3:** Start documentation from day one and keep everything organized since writing things clearly as you go makes design reviews, team reports, and final deliverables far less stressful.
4. **Recommendation 4:** Communicate with your team consistently and check your system compatibility often because even small misunderstandings about connectors, pinouts, or power setups can cause major delays later in the project.
5. **Recommendation 5:** Adopt a mindset of testing early and iterating more often than not because hardware rarely works perfectly on the first try and making steady and small improvements will lead to a far more reliable final system.
